short description

Shotmaker is golf played with one finger. Your swipe is your swing, so its speed, line and rhythm decide how far the ball flies and which way it curves. There are no meters and no luck: the same swing always hits the same shot. Play five courses from a parkland classic to a mountain valley, shape every shot, and build a handicap that means the same thing for every player.

about 70 words

long description

Most golf games ask you to stop a moving bar. Shotmaker asks you to swing. You draw your swing on a pad at the foot of the screen, and the game measures it in millimetres the way a launch monitor measures a real one: the speed of the swipe, the line it travels, the face it presents and how cleanly it strikes. The ball then flies through real air, with drag and spin worked out from the same physics that launch monitors use. Its carry, launch and spin for a well-struck shot match the averages of tour professionals.

There are no random numbers anywhere in the swing or the flight. Identical swings give identical shots, so every miss belongs to the player, and so does every improvement. The game spans the range from a 28 handicap to a plus 5. A new player hits it shorter and more crooked, and tour numbers come only with practice.

Handicap rounds are played at the same settings and in each course's own fixed wind for everyone, everywhere, and nothing in the settings can change them. A handicap in Shotmaker therefore means the same for every player. Players can watch it fall on a chart, look back at every scorecard, and see where shots are won and lost: fairways, greens, putts, up and downs, sand saves and the carry of each club.

Shotmaker opens with five courses. Larchmere is a parkland course and Dunmarra Links a seaside links, and both are free for ever. Cormorant Point is a clifftop course above the ocean, Cinder Mesa runs through a red-rock desert and Glacier Falls lies in a mountain valley. All three are in the Shotmaker Club, and each can also be bought on its own. A caddie reads each hole and suggests the play, and a practice ground and eleven lessons teach the game from rhythm to reading the wind.

Shotmaker is made by Toayen, a small independent studio in the United Kingdom.

features

  • Your swipe is your swing. Speed, line, face and strike are each controlled by the finger, with no meters or timing buttons.
  • Real ball flight. A drag and spin model tuned to tour launch-monitor averages, with wind, firm ground, roll and run-out.
  • No luck. The same swing always hits the same shot, so a player's spread of shots tightens as they practise.
  • Shape every shot. Players can draw it, fade it, flight it low under the wind or float a wedge onto a firm green.
  • A handicap that means something. Every handicap round is played in the same fixed conditions for every player.
  • Five courses, ninety holes. Two are free for ever and three are in the Shotmaker Club.
  • A caddie. The caddie reads the hole, the lie and the player's own spread of shots, then suggests the play in plain words.
  • Stats. The Stats page shows form over time, fairways, greens, putting, the short game and every club in the bag.
  • A practice ground. It has a range with targets, putting, chipping and pitching greens, and eleven lessons.
  • Private by design. There is no account and no tracking, and nothing leaves the phone.

the courses

picture slotLarchmere, a tee shot over water
free for ever

larchmere

A parkland classic of rolling fairways, still lakes and old woods. Larchmere is where every player starts, and it rewards a well-placed drive more than a long one. Trees play exactly as they are drawn, and the lakes catch anything careless.

narrow water · the island · the thimble

picture slotDunmarra Links, the sea beyond the dunes
free for ever

dunmarra links

A wild seaside links on a bright, breezy afternoon. Firm, fast turf runs over old dunes, deep pot bunkers hide from the tee and gorse waits beside the landing areas. The wind and the ground decide the shot, so the ball has to be kept low and allowed to run.

the stamp · seawall · twice burn

picture slotCormorant Point, a carry over the ocean
in the club

cormorant point

Three landscapes in one round. Cormorant Point starts in a forest of tall dark pines, breaks out over white dunes and ice plant, then runs along the clifftops above turquoise coves. Its white bunkers are torn into capes and bays, and on the run home the ball is carried over the ocean itself.

long pines · the ledge · the chasm

picture slotCinder Mesa, emerald fairway through red rock
in the club

cinder mesa

A clear morning in red-rock desert. Emerald fairways run as ribbons through red sand and sagebrush, past sandstone domes and across a field of black lava. The last holes drop through canyons to a creek below the clubhouse. The air is still, so this is target golf where only the carry counts.

the kiln · stepping stones · freefall

picture slotGlacier Falls, the river and the peaks
in the club · new

glacier falls

A summer's day in a mountain valley. Striped fairways roll between walls of dark spruce, a milky turquoise river runs beside them, and limestone peaks fill the sky. Some tees stand high on forest benches so the drive drops to the valley floor, and the round ends under a timber-and-stone lodge beside a falling stream.

the cauldron · glacier run · the falls

the shotmaker club

Larchmere and Dunmarra Links are free for ever, with the practice ground, the lessons and the handicap. The Shotmaker Club opens Cormorant Point, Cinder Mesa and Glacier Falls, and every course added after launch. It costs £24.99 a year or £3.99 a month, with a free week to start, and Family Sharing is on. A player who would rather not subscribe can buy any Club course for £5.99 and keep it. Nothing bought in Shotmaker changes how the ball flies or what a handicap round asks of a player.
